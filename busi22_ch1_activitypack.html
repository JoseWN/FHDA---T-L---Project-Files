Economic System Sort

Classify real-world policies into the right economic system

⏱ 12 min 👥 Pairs LO 1.3 · LO 1.4

Instructions

  1. Read each scenario card below.
  2. Click (or circle on print) whether it best describes a Market, Command, or Mixed economy.
  3. Write one sentence justifying your choice.
📊

Scenario A: The government sets the price of bread and gasoline, and allocates how much each factory must produce each year.

Justification: ___
✅ Command Economy. Government controls prices and production quotas — the defining feature of a command economy (North Korea, historical USSR).
🏥

Scenario B: Healthcare is provided by a mix of private hospitals and government Medicare/Medicaid programs. Prices in private markets are largely set by supply and demand, but the government regulates insurance.

Justification: ___
✅ Mixed Economy. Both private markets and government programs coexist — this is the real U.S. healthcare system. Perfect example of a mixed economy.
🚗

Scenario C: A used car dealer sets their own prices based on what buyers are willing to pay. If nobody buys at $10,000, they drop to $8,500 until the car sells.

Justification: ___
✅ Market Economy. Price is set by supply and demand — the dealer adjusts price until the market clears. This is equilibrium in action (LO 1.4).
⚡

Scenario D: California's Public Utilities Commission sets maximum rates that PG&E can charge for electricity, even though PG&E is a private company.

Justification: ___
✅ Mixed Economy. Private company, but government-regulated pricing. This is how utilities work in the U.S. — regulated markets, not pure markets or command.

Key teaching points: (1) Stakeholder interests genuinely conflict — there's no option that pleases everyone. (2) The form of ownership (LO 1.7) directly determines what options are even available — a sole proprietor can't easily raise capital like a corporation can. (3) The environmental forces (LO 1.5) are what's driving the crisis: economic pressure, technological disruption (Amazon Fresh), and social factors (employee welfare, community impact) are all at play simultaneously.

Chapter Check-for-Understanding

Individual rapid-fire quiz — whole class

⏱ 12 min 👤 Individual All 8 LOs

Answer each question on your own, then we'll review as a class. No notes.

Q1 (LO 1.1): A local bakery earns $12,000/month in revenue and has $9,500 in costs. Its profit is ___. Is this a successful business? What does this profit signal to the market?

Profit = $2,500/mo. Yes — it's covering costs and generating a return. The profit signals: "this community values this bakery — resources should keep flowing here." If it lost money, it would signal the opposite.

Q2 (LO 1.2): A food delivery startup has: a software algorithm (valued at $2M), a team of 8 developers, 200 gig drivers, leased warehouse space, and an angel investor who took the risk. Name the factor of production for each.

Algorithm → Knowledge; Developers → Labor; Drivers → Labor; Warehouse space → Land; Angel investor → Entrepreneurship (and the funding = Capital). Note: the algorithm being worth $2M while maybe costing $80K to build illustrates why Knowledge creates outsized value.

Q3 (LO 1.3): What three questions does every economic system answer? How does a market economy answer question #1 differently from a command economy?

The 3 questions: What to produce? How to produce it? For whom? In a market economy, Q1 is answered by profit signals (businesses produce what people will pay for). In a command economy, the government decides what gets produced regardless of what people want — which often leads to shortages and surpluses.

Q4 (LO 1.5): Tesla is developing a new battery that doubles EV range at half the cost. Name two environmental forces this impacts and how.

Technological (disrupts existing battery supply chains, auto industry), Competitive/Global (intensifies competition with Chinese EV makers like BYD), Economic (could shift consumer spending toward EVs, away from gas vehicles), Natural (reduces emissions, impacts oil demand), Legal/Regulatory (could trigger new EV incentive legislation or new safety standards). Most answers accepted if reasoning is sound.

Q5 (LO 1.7): Your friend starts a photography business as a sole proprietor. She's successful and is sued when her photos are used without consent. What's the biggest financial risk to her? How would forming an LLC or corporation change this?

As a sole proprietor, she has unlimited personal liability — the plaintiff can go after her personal bank account, car, home, savings. As an LLC or corporation, liability is limited to business assets only — her personal assets are protected. This is often the #1 reason successful sole proprietors eventually incorporate.
